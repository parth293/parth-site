## Funding and admission

The honest headline here: TDM PhDs mostly sit inside Clinical Pharmacy or
Medical departments, and the strongest career track (hospital clinical
pharmacology) is usually filled by PharmD or MD candidates, not engineers.
The only track that's actually viable for me is computational
model-informed precision dosing (MIPD) — Bayesian dose optimisation, which
is essentially applied pharmacometrics — using existing population PK
models rather than running a hospital-based clinical study myself.
Funding is described as more clinically sourced (hospital quality-
improvement budgets, PCORI) than the NIH/industry combination the other
subfields get.

## AI and automation risk

Framed as low-moderate and, notably, in my favour if I'm on the right side
of it: the commercial MIPD products (InsightRx, DoseMe) are themselves
essentially the "AI" being built, on top of population PK models — so a
computational MIPD PhD is building that tooling, not being displaced by
it.

## Career exit

Called good but narrower than pharmacometrics specifically because the
highest-value clinical roles need credentials (PharmD, MD) I don't have.
The accessible roles — scientist at an MIPD software company, clinical
pharmacology scientist at pharma with a TDM focus — are described as a
subset of what a straight pharmacometrics PhD already opens, which is the
core argument against this specific direction versus just doing
pharmacometrics directly.

## Immigration

Australia is flagged as unusually strong here specifically — the
University of Queensland and a national MIPD research centre are named as
disproportionately significant relative to Australia's size — otherwise
the same general pattern as the rest of the set, with the note that US
hospital-based clinical roles often need PR/citizenship for credentialing.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: InsightRx and DoseMe both have free/demo tiers, and
implementing a published population PK model for a classic TDM drug
(tacrolimus, vancomycin) in nlmixr2 for individual Bayesian dose estimation
is a realistic artifact. Described as harder than the pharmacometrics
equivalent specifically because there isn't a comparable India-based
bridge-role landscape (Syngene, IQVIA-style CRO roles) for this narrower
specialty.
