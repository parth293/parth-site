numbers, state of my thinking on the date above.

## Funding and admission

Called the best-funded computational subfield in pharmaceutical sciences —
NIH, FDA's Critical Path Initiative, and standing (not just project-based)
industry partnerships with major pharma companies are all cited. Admission
is described as favourable for exactly my profile: quantitative
engineering background plus real industry experience, in a smaller
applicant pool than mainstream pharmacology draws. Uppsala University
(Sweden) is named repeatedly as the world's leading program (Mats
Karlsson); University of Florida, SUNY Buffalo, and UCSF also named as
strong US options. None of this is verified against current admissions
data.

## AI and automation risk

Argued as low, and specifically framed as AI creating more demand for
skilled pharmacometricians rather than less — the claim is that AI
automates routine diagnostics (goodness-of-fit plots) while the emerging,
scarce, well-compensated profile is someone who can combine mechanistic
PK/PD modelling with ML fluency, which is described as closer to my actual
background (tech + pharma) than a typical biology-track applicant's.

## Career exit

Framed as the best industry exit in pharmaceutical sciences, on the
argument that pharmacometrics is both non-discretionary (every drug
company needs it for regulatory reasons) and strategically central (it
informs go/no-go and dosing decisions, not just supports them). No postdoc
required is repeated as the clearest, most consistent claim across every
note in this set. Certara, and biotech specifically (as the founding
pharmacometrician at an early-stage company), are both named as strong
paths — the biotech version is the one that aligns most with my own
startup background.

## Immigration

Same general shape as the rest of the set, with the addition that
Uppsala/Sweden is floated as a specific, less-competed European option
given the program's reputation, alongside the usual Canada-first
recommendation.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: nlmixr2 (free R package) as a NONMEM alternative, public FDA
pharmacokinetic datasets, and replicating-and-extending a published PopPK
analysis are all named as realistic, checkable pre-application work —
closest in spirit, again, to how I actually build the grad-school
trackers, and the one part of this whole evaluation I'd trust myself to
verify directly rather than take on faith.
