## Funding and admission

Funding is described as moderate — narrower than pharmacometrics, sourced
more from FDA's Office of Generic Drugs (through GDUFA, the Generic Drug
User Fee Act) and the generic-pharma industry than from broad NIH streams.
The interesting claim here is the admission angle: my actual manufacturing
background — SUPAC changes, dissolution testing, having seen BA/BE data
packages reviewed by regulators — is framed as the single strongest direct
match of any subfield in this whole set, not just "relevant." The risk
flagged is being seen as "too close" to the work already, without the
research depth a PhD is supposed to add.

## AI and automation risk

Described as the highest disruption risk of any PK/PD subfield here —
routine bioequivalence study execution and standard statistical analysis
(the TOST test) are said to be automating faster than elsewhere, while
complex-product BE (inhaled, topical) and IVIVC modelling stay
AI-resistant. If this direction, the positioning inside it — computational
IVIVC over routine study execution — is described as mattering more than
in any other subfield.

## Career exit

Called good, not excellent — a narrower ceiling than pharmacometrics or
PBPK, and dominated by generic pharma (Teva, Sun Pharma, Dr. Reddy's) and
FDA's Office of Generic Drugs rather than big-pharma innovation roles or
startups. One notable claim: this is the one PK/PD subfield where staying
in India afterward is described as not a dead end, given India's generic
industry — a genuine alternative if my own plans changed.

## Immigration

The FDA's Office of Generic Drugs — a plausible target employer here —
doesn't sponsor H1B, so that specific path needs PR first, unlike most
industry roles in the other notes. New Jersey's generic-pharma cluster is
named as sponsoring reliably. Otherwise the same general pattern as the
rest of the set.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: GastroPlus has a free demo, FDA's actual BA/BE guidance
documents are public, and the R package PKNCA handles standard
bioequivalence statistics on public data. Described as the fastest
pre-application ramp of any subfield here, specifically because my current
job already overlaps with it rather than requiring new ground to be built
from scratch.
