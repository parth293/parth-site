## Funding and admission

This doesn't exist as its own PhD program — it lives inside a broader
pharmacometrics or clinical pharmacology program, with the advisor's
specific focus (TMDD, biologics PK) mattering more than the program name.
Funding within those programs is said to be good, with biologics-focused
TMDD labs specifically well funded by biotech industry money right now.
The stated advantage for me: this needs heavier differential-equations
comfort than plain PopPK, and IIT BHU's engineering maths curriculum is a
real, checkable asset here — ODEs and numerical methods were actually part
of my coursework, unlike most biology-track applicants.

## AI and automation risk

The argument is that this field is harder to automate than routine PopPK
specifically because there's no standard model structure — every drug's
nonlinearity has a different mechanistic basis, and TMDD models for a new
biologic require genuine biological reasoning to set up. Plausible, not
verified.

## Career exit

Framed as excellent with a specific premium for TMDD/biologics/ADC
expertise, because supply is described as scarce relative to a fast-growing
demand (antibody-drug conjugates, bispecifics, gene therapy all need this).
Target roles: TMDD modeller, biologic PK/PD scientist, ADC
pharmacokineticist. Boston/Cambridge named repeatedly as the geographic
centre of gravity.

## Immigration

Same general pattern as the rest of this set — Canada most predictable,
US strongest ceiling with the EB backlog caveat, Switzerland (specifically
Roche/Novartis in Basel) named as the highest-paying but hardest-to-access
option for a non-EU national.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: mrgsolve (free R package) for ODE-based PK simulation, and
replicating a published TMDD model for a monoclonal antibody with public
parameters, are both realistic evening-and-weekend projects — described as
harder than the equivalent PopPK exercise because of the extra maths, but
achievable in a few months.
