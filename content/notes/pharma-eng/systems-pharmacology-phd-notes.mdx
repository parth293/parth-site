## Funding and admission

Described as very highly funded — NIH's LINCS programme, FDA funding for
QSP methodology specifically, and aggressive industry investment
(AstraZeneca named repeatedly as the most QSP-forward pharma company) are
all cited. Admission is framed as unusually open to a non-traditional
profile, since the field explicitly recruits from engineering,
mathematics, and computer science alongside biology — more so than
classical pharmacology programs. The honest gap, more than in any other
note in this set: essentially zero cell-biology or molecular-biology
background on my side, which is described as a real 12–18 month learning
curve, not a minor one. Leiden University (Piet van der Graaf) and MIT
(Douglas Lauffenburger) are named as the field's most prominent labs — not
verified.

## AI and automation risk

Framed as low, with an interesting specific claim: systems pharmacology
and AI are described as converging rather than competing — AlphaFold-style
structural predictions feed better systems models, and systems models give
AI predictions mechanistic grounding they otherwise lack. The emerging
"physics-informed ML" niche is flagged as exactly the kind of hybrid
profile my tech background could plausibly support, more than a
biology-track student's.

## Career exit

Called excellent, with the important caveat that postdoc expectations are
higher here than in pharmacometrics — academic systems pharmacology has a
stronger postdoc culture, though industry QSP roles at large pharma are
still described as directly accessible from a PhD. The startup angle is
flagged as the strongest of any subfield in this set: an early-stage
oncology or gene-therapy biotech is described as often having nobody in
house who can build a systems model at all, which is a real echo of the
"join early and own the function" pattern from my own Leucine experience.

## Immigration

Same general shape as the rest of the set, plus a specific note: Leiden
(Netherlands) and the Dutch highly-skilled-migrant visa are floated as an
underused option specifically for QSP, alongside AstraZeneca's Cambridge
QSP group as a UK anchor point.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: Python plus scipy/numpy, the free BioModels Database of
published biological models, and the free COPASI simulation software are
all named as a realistic starting stack — described as needing real
biology self-study alongside the computational work (Uri Alon's free
Coursera systems-biology course was named specifically), which is a
heavier lift than the equivalent pharmacometrics or PBPK ramp.
