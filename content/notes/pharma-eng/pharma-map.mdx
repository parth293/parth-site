Ask a formulation scientist and a pharmacovigilance officer what they do all
day and you'd struggle to guess they work in the same industry. One is
choosing a coating polymer so a tablet survives stomach acid; the other is
reading adverse-event reports seven years after that tablet shipped.
"Pharmaceutical sciences" is not one field — it's roughly thirteen, loosely
federated under a name that promises more coherence than the field actually
has.

I'm using this page as the index for the rest of this pillar. It lists the
domains, with a one-line sense of what each one actually does, and links out
wherever I've written the note — which right now means one branch,
pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, because that's the corner I've spent
the most time in while deciding whether to go do a PhD in it. Everything
else on this page is scaffolding: a name and a placeholder, not a claim that
I understand it. I'll keep adding branches and links as I go deeper into
other domains, so treat this as dated, not finished.

## Medicinal chemistry

Designing and synthesising the drug molecule itself — structure-activity
relationships, computational molecular modelling, fragment-based drug
discovery, prodrug design. The "what molecule do we even make" layer,
upstream of everything else here.

## Pharmacology

How a drug interacts with the body at the mechanism level — receptor
binding, signalling cascades, systems-level network behaviour. Includes
[systems pharmacology](/notes/pharma-eng/systems-pharmacology), which is
where this domain starts overlapping with PK/PD below: instead of one
target, one effect, it treats a drug's effect as a perturbation to a whole
biological network.

## Pharmaceutics and drug delivery

Turning a molecule into a product a patient can actually take: tablet and
capsule design, controlled-release systems, nanoparticle and targeted
delivery, transdermal and pulmonary routes. Biopharmaceutics — how the
product's physical form governs how much drug actually reaches the
bloodstream — sits here too, and connects directly into bioavailability
work below.

## Pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics (PK/PD)

What the body does to a drug, and what the drug does to the body, expressed
quantitatively. This is the branch I've actually written up:

- [ADME](/notes/pharma-eng/adme) — absorption, distribution, metabolism,
  excretion: the four-stage obstacle course every drug runs before it can do
  anything useful.
- [Population pharmacokinetics](/notes/pharma-eng/population-pharmacokinetics) —
  why the same dose behaves differently across a real, diverse population,
  and how to model that statistically.
- [Nonlinear PK modelling](/notes/pharma-eng/nonlinear-pk-modeling) — what
  happens once a drug saturates the systems that clear it, and the maths
  that stops working when it does.
- [Drug-drug interactions](/notes/pharma-eng/drug-drug-interactions) — how
  one drug changes what another drug does, usually through a shared liver
  enzyme.
- [PBPK modelling](/notes/pharma-eng/pbpk-modeling) — building a virtual
  human body, organ by organ, and simulating a drug's path through it
  instead of fitting an abstract compartment model.
- [Pharmacometrics](/notes/pharma-eng/pharmacometrics) — the integrating
  discipline that uses all of the above to answer one question: what dose,
  for which patient, at what stage of disease.
- [Bioavailability and bioequivalence](/notes/pharma-eng/bioavailability-bioequivalence) —
  the science that decides whether a generic tablet is allowed to be called
  the same drug as the branded one.
- [Therapeutic drug monitoring](/notes/pharma-eng/therapeutic-drug-monitoring) —
  measuring a drug's level in an actual patient's blood and adjusting the
  dose in real time, for the drugs where guessing by bodyweight isn't safe
  enough.

## Pharmacognosy and natural products

Medicines from natural sources — phytochemistry, ethnopharmacology, marine
and microbial natural products, traditional medicine systems. Not a domain
I've looked into yet.

## Pharmaceutical biotechnology

Biologics: recombinant proteins, monoclonal antibodies, gene and cell
therapy, mRNA, biosimilars, vaccine development, and the upstream/downstream
bioprocessing that manufactures them. This is where I'd expect the most
overlap with the [bioprocess-eng](/notes/bioprocess-eng) pillar once I write
it up — not started here yet.

## Toxicology

Preclinical safety assessment, genotoxicity, organ-specific and
reproductive toxicology, regulatory toxicology, risk assessment. Not
started.

## Clinical pharmacy

Patient-facing drug therapy management — oncology, infectious disease,
paediatric, geriatric, and critical care pharmacy practice. Not started.

## Pharmaceutical analysis and quality

The measurement layer: chromatography and spectroscopy, bioanalytical
chemistry, stability and dissolution testing, quality control, GLP. Not
started, though it borders GMP territory I already know from six years at
Leucine — likely an early candidate for expansion.

## Regulatory affairs and drug policy

Approval pathways (FDA, EMA, ICH), GMP, pharmacovigilance, IP and patent
law, orphan drug and generic drug policy. Also close to what I already know
from work — another likely early expansion.

## Pharmaceutical microbiology

Sterility and endotoxin testing, microbial contamination control, aseptic
processing, antibiotic and antiviral drug research. Not started.

## Pharmacoepidemiology and pharmacoeconomics

Population-level drug use, real-world evidence, adverse drug reaction
surveillance, cost-effectiveness and health economics. Not started.

## Social and behavioural pharmacy

Medication adherence, patient communication, health literacy, access to
medicines. Not started.
