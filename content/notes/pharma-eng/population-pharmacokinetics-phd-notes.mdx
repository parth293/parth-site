## Funding and admission

Funding is described as very high — NIH, FDA's Critical Path Initiative,
and direct industry partnerships all cited — with the reasoning that
NONMEM licences and compute mean labs tend to be well-resourced rather than
scraping for equipment. Admission is framed as moderate and favourable for
a quantitative non-traditional profile like mine, with the honest gap being
no publications and no direct modelling experience yet — something a
few months of self-taught nlmixr2/Monolix work could partly close before
applying.

## AI and automation risk

The claim here is that AI is mostly automating the lowest-skill parts —
routine covariate screening, standard diagnostic plots — while the
higher-skill parts (deciding model structure, defending assumptions to a
regulator, interpreting what a covariate means clinically) stay human. I
can't verify that split; it's a reasonable-sounding argument, not a
measurement.

## Career exit

Described as one of the strongest direct PhD-to-industry pipelines in
pharmaceutical sciences — pharmacometricians are said to be scarce
relative to demand, and Certara plus every major pharma company's
DMPK/clinical pharmacology department are named as reliable employers, no
postdoc typically required. Target roles: pharmacometrician, clinical
pharmacology scientist, PKPD modeller.

## Immigration

Same general shape as the other PK/PD notes: Canada looks most
predictable, US has the best career ceiling but the EB backlog for Indian
nationals, UK/Germany/Switzerland all plausible but with their own
friction. Not verified against current policy.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely checkable: nlmixr2 and Monolix both have free tiers, NONMEM
example datasets (theophylline, warfarin) are standard teaching data
anyone can pull and model, and replicating a published PopPK analysis on
public FDA pharmacokinetic data is a realistic few-months artifact — this
is the one part of the whole evaluation I'd actually trust myself to
execute and verify directly, rather than take on faith.
