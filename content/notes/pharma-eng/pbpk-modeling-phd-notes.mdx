## Funding and admission

Described as very highly funded — FDA's Critical Path Initiative treats
PBPK as an explicit regulatory-science priority, and Simcyp/GastroPlus
licences reportedly cost enough (tens of thousands a year) that labs using
them tend to be institutionally well-resourced rather than scraping.
Admission is called favourable for an engineering background specifically,
because building a PBPK model — defining compartments, flows, and their
interactions — is described as structurally close to systems engineering.
Amin Rostami-Hodjegan's group at Manchester was named repeatedly as the
world's leading pediatric-PBPK lab and a Simcyp co-founder; I haven't
verified current lab composition or whether he's taking students.

## AI and automation risk

Argued as low, on the reasoning that PBPK's whole value is predicting
behaviour in situations with no data yet (a pediatric patient, a novel
modality) — exactly where a data-driven ML model has nothing to learn
from. AI is framed as useful for estimating input parameters (predicting
lipophilicity or protein binding from structure), not for replacing the
mechanistic model itself.

## Career exit

Framed as exceptional — among the top exit profiles in pharma sciences,
alongside pharmacometrics, with the FDA's explicit acceptance of PBPK
models in place of certain clinical studies cited as the structural reason
demand is durable. Both big pharma (Roche, Novartis, Pfizer) and biotech
(specifically mRNA/gene-therapy companies that don't yet have a mature PBPK
framework for their own modality) are named as live options — the biotech
angle is the more startup-compatible version of this career, closer to my
own background.

## Immigration

Same general shape as the rest of the set, with one addition: Manchester
(via the Rostami-Hodjegan connection) and Monash (Melbourne) are both named
as PBPK-specific programs with unusually strong direct industry pipelines
in their own countries, which is a more specific claim than the generic
"UK is fine, Australia is fine" pattern in the other notes.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: PK-Sim is free, open source, and maintained by Bayer and the
Open Systems Pharmacology community, with published step-by-step
tutorials. Replicating a PBPK model for a well-characterised drug
(midazolam is the standard CYP3A4 substrate example) and validating it
against public clinical data is described as a realistic few-months
artifact — closer in spirit to how I actually build the grad-school
trackers than most of this evaluation.
