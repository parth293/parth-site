## Funding and admission

Funding is described as moderate-good rather than exceptional — real NIH
and FDA Critical Path money, plus industry funding that's more
project-based than the standing lab partnerships pharmacometrics labs are
said to have. University of Washington is repeatedly named as the world's
leading DDI research group (Ken Thummel, Danny Shen were named — I haven't
verified who's currently active there). My regulatory audit background is
flagged as specifically relevant, since DDI regulatory submissions are a
narrow, scrutinised area where understanding what FDA actually wants is
unusual for a PhD applicant to already know.

## AI and automation risk

Moderate: the claim is that high-throughput CYP-inhibition screening is
being automated, while mechanistic PBPK-based DDI prediction and novel
transporter/biologic mechanisms are not, because there's little training
data for AI to learn from in those cases.

## Career exit

Called good but with a narrower ceiling than pharmacometrics or PBPK —
DDI scientists are described as a specialised function within DMPK rather
than the strategic centre of a drug program. Certara/Simcyp is named
specifically as a strong entry point (DDI prediction is core Simcyp
business). Skews toward big pharma over biotech, since DDI risk management
is usually outsourced by smaller companies to CROs rather than built
in-house.

## Immigration

Same general shape as the rest of the set. One specific note: Certara is
named as a reliable H1B sponsor for this specialisation, and Seattle's
biotech ecosystem (given the UW connection above) is flagged as unusually
sponsorship-friendly if that lab connection panned out.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

Concretely: Certara offers a free student version of Simcyp, and reading
FDA's actual DDI guidance documents closely enough to discuss them
intelligently is described as a real differentiator most applicants skip.
A data-analysis paper mining FDA's public label database for DDI patterns
in a specific drug class (oncology combinations, say) is floated as a
realistic few-month project — I haven't attempted it.
