the state of my thinking on the date above, not a conclusion, and not
something to cite.

## Funding and admission

NIH funding for ADME-adjacent work looks abundant on paper (NIGMS, NCI,
NIDDK), and pharma companies routinely sponsor academic ADME labs
directly, which suggests a fully funded PhD is realistic at an R1 program —
I haven't verified specific stipend numbers against a real offer. My
admission case rests on framing the GMP/regulatory background as genuinely
relevant — manufacturing scientists care about excipients and stability
affecting bioavailability — rather than pretending it substitutes for lab
experience, which it doesn't. Computational ADME (Python, PBPK software
like Simcyp or the free PK-Sim, cheminformatics) is the natural entry
point for me; wet-lab ADME would mean starting the experimental side from
zero.

## AI and automation risk

Routine, high-throughput ADME screening is genuinely being automated by ML
property-prediction tools. The judgement-heavy part — diagnosing why a
prediction fails in a novel chemical space, understanding transporter
effects, reconciling species differences — looks like it stays human for a
while, but I'm relying on general reasoning about the field here, not a
labour-market study.

## Career exit

DMPK (drug metabolism and pharmacokinetics) is described as one of the more
reliable PhD-to-industry pipelines in pharma sciences, largely because
every drug company needs the function for regulatory reasons — that's a
plausible structural argument, not something I've verified against actual
hiring data. Target roles: DMPK scientist, in vitro ADME scientist, PBPK
modeller, translational ADME scientist. A postdoc doesn't look required for
the computational track specifically.

## Immigration

Canada reads as the most predictable path (Express Entry, no per-country
backlog); the US has the strongest career ceiling but the well-known
multi-year EB-2/EB-3 backlog for Indian nationals. Neither of these is
something I've run past an immigration lawyer.

## Pre-PhD credibility building

The concrete, checkable version of this: PK-Sim is free and open source,
there are FDA guidance documents I could actually read and discuss
intelligently, and a replicated-and-extended PopPK-style analysis on public
data is a realistic pre-application artifact within a few months of
evening work — closer to how I actually build the grad-school trackers than
anything else in this note.
