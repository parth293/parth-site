This is the canonical version. It changes as my daughter grows and as I learn
what actually worked, but at any point there is exactly one of these — new
thinking goes in a dated post that argues for a change, and once I've made the
change, this page is what gets updated. See
[why fifteen, and how this list works](/notes/life-eng/why-fifteen) for the
reasoning behind the structure itself.

**Target: functionally independent by fifteen.** Not eighteen. The legal age
of adulthood is a compliance date, not a competence date, and I'd rather
under-shoot and keep adding than plan to a deadline that lets everyone relax
for the last three years.

**Principles and skills, not facts.** Nothing on this list is knowledge for
its own sake. Every entry is something she can act on, and every entry should
translate into a real decision she'll actually face — what to eat, how to
treat someone who's wronged her, whether to pick up a cigarette. What she gets
curious about beyond this is hers to choose; I'm not assigning trivia.

## 0–4

- **The golden rule, modelled before it's explained.** Treat others as you
  would want to be treated. At this age it isn't a lesson, it's what she sees
  me do when I'm frustrated, tired, or dealing with someone who's rude to me.
- **A daily reading habit**, even before she's reading herself — being read to
  is what builds the appetite the skill later runs on.

## 5–8

- **Independent reading fluency.** Not a specific book list — the bare-minimum
  muscle so that whatever she wants to go deep on later, self-taught, is
  available to her. This is the one skill that unlocks every other skill.
- **Times tables to 100.** Arithmetic fast enough to be invisible, so that
  later math is limited by the new idea, not by recomputing 7×8 every time it
  shows up.
- **The golden rule, explained.** Why it works, not just that it's the rule —
  the mechanism (people mostly treat you the way you treat them, over enough
  time) rather than the platitude.

## 9–11

- **Basic cooking.** Enough to feed herself a real meal without help — not
  recipes memorised, but the underlying logic of why a dish works, so she can
  improvise.
- **Basic nutrition.** What a diet needs and why, so food choices later are
  informed rather than inherited or accidental.

## 12–15

- **Fitness as a habit, not an event.** Why regular movement matters, before
  the version of her that has to decide this for herself without a parent
  reminding her shows up.
- **Basic anatomy — the parts that fail first.** Muscles, tendons, ligaments,
  and specifically posture, because bad posture compounds quietly for decades
  before it becomes a real problem, and the earlier the habit is right the
  cheaper it is to keep right.
- **Why the common bad habits are bad, mechanistically.** Smoking, drinking —
  not "these are bad for you" as an assertion, but what they actually do,
  stated plainly enough that avoiding them is a reasoned decision and not just
  obedience to a rule she'll drop the moment no one's watching.

---

**Open, not yet placed:** items I know belong somewhere on this list but
haven't decided which milestone they belong to — money and saving, how to
recognise when someone is taking advantage of her, basic self-defence, how to
disagree with someone without ending the relationship. These will move into a
milestone once I've thought them through properly rather than sequencing them
by guess.
