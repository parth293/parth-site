## Atomic number and mass number

- **Atomic number (Z)** — proton count. Identifies the element; fixes its
  position on the periodic table.
- **Mass number (A)** — protons + neutrons. Electrons are ignored
  (negligible mass).

### Isotopes of hydrogen — same Z, different A

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons | Mass number |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protium (¹H) | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| Deuterium (²H, D) | 1 | 1 | 2 |
| Tritium (³H, T) | 1 | 2 | 3 |

## Water — structure and polarity

- **Molecular weight** — 18 daltons (2 from its two H atoms + 16 from O).
- **Bond angle** — H–O–H is 104.5°.
- Bent shape + oxygen's higher electronegativity → uneven charge
  distribution → water is **polar**.
- **CO₂ is not polar** — linear and symmetrical, so its two C=O bond
  dipoles point opposite and cancel exactly.

### Why its properties look "too high" for its size

- Unusually high melting point, boiling point, heat of vaporization, and
  surface tension for an 18-dalton molecule.
- Cause: **intermolecular hydrogen bonding** between water molecules —
  breaking enough of these bonds to melt, boil, or vaporize it takes far
  more energy than the low molecular weight alone would suggest.

## Water as a solvent

- Water is the **universal solvent** — being polar, it dissolves most
  polar and ionic compounds.
- **Hydrophilic** — polar; dissolves in water.
- **Hydrophobic** — nonpolar; virtually insoluble in water.
- **Amphipathic** compounds have both a polar and a nonpolar region in the
  same molecule — the hydrophilic region interacts with water, the
  hydrophobic region avoids it.
- **Dielectric constant** — a solvent's capacity to neutralize the
  attraction between opposite electrical charges.
  - Water has a high dielectric constant → weakens the attraction between
    oppositely charged ions (e.g. Na⁺ and Cl⁻) → makes water a powerful
    solvent for salts.

## Water — acid/base behavior

- **Amphoteric** — a substance that can act as both an acid and a base.
- **Amphiprotic** — a substance that can either donate or accept a proton
  (H⁺). Water is both amphoteric and amphiprotic.

### Ionization of water

$$
H_2O \rightleftharpoons H^+ + OH^-
$$

- $H_2O$ — water
- $H^+$ — hydrogen ion (proton)
- $OH^-$ — hydroxide ion

### Autoionization of water

$$
2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-
$$

- $H_3O^+$ — hydronium ion, water's conjugate acid
- $OH^-$ — hydroxide ion, water's conjugate base

- Degree of ionization is measured by **electrical conductivity**.
- In pure water, $[H^+] = 10^{-7}$ mol/L — i.e. 1 L of pure water contains
  $10^{-7}$ mol of H⁺ ions.
- On electrolysis, $H_3O^+$ (cation) migrates to the **cathode**; $OH^-$
  (anion) migrates to the **anode**.

### Equilibrium constant of water's ionization

$$
K_{eq} = \dfrac{[H^+][OH^-]}{[H_2O]}
$$

- $K_{eq}$ — equilibrium constant of water's ionization, mol/L
- $[H^+]$ — hydrogen ion concentration, mol/L
- $[OH^-]$ — hydroxide ion concentration, mol/L
- $[H_2O]$ — molar concentration of water, mol/L

- Water's degree of autoionization is very small, so $[H_2O]$ is treated as
  constant: $55.5$ mol/L.
- $K_{eq}$ at 25°C = $1.8 \times 10^{-16}$ mol/L.

### Ion product of water ($K_w$)

$$
K_w = [H^+][OH^-]
$$

- $K_w$ — ion product of water: product of $[H^+]$ and $[OH^-]$ in pure
  water, mol²/L²
- $[H^+]$ — hydrogen ion concentration, mol/L
- $[OH^-]$ — hydroxide ion concentration, mol/L

- $K_w = K_{eq} \times 55.5$ (rolling $[H_2O]$'s constant concentration into
  $K_{eq}$).
- $K_w$ at 25°C = $10^{-14}$ mol²/L².

### Acidic, basic, and neutral solutions

| Solution | $[H^+]$ / $[H_3O^+]$ |
| --- | --- |
| Acidic | $> 10^{-7}$ M |
| Neutral | $= 10^{-7}$ M |
| Basic | $< 10^{-7}$ M |

### pH

$$
pH = -\log[H^+]
$$

- $pH$ — measure of acidity, unitless
- $[H^+]$ — hydrogen ion concentration, mol/L

## Key points

- Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.
- Protium, deuterium, tritium = hydrogen's isotopes, with 0, 1, 2 neutrons.
- Water: 18 daltons, bent at 104.5°, polar. CO₂: linear, symmetrical,
  nonpolar.
- Water's unusually high mp/bp/heat of vaporization/surface tension come
  from intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
- Hydrophilic = polar = water-soluble. Hydrophobic = nonpolar =
  water-insoluble. Amphipathic = both, in one molecule.
- High dielectric constant → weaker ionic attraction → water dissolves
  salts easily.
- Water is amphoteric (acts as acid or base) and amphiprotic (donates or
  accepts H⁺): $H_2O \rightleftharpoons H^+ + OH^-$, and
  $2H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_3O^+ + OH^-$. $H_3O^+$ → cathode, $OH^-$ →
  anode.
- $K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = K_{eq} \times 55.5 = 10^{-14}$ mol²/L² at 25°C
  ($K_{eq} = 1.8 \times 10^{-16}$ mol/L; $[H_2O]$ held constant at $55.5$
  mol/L).
- Pure water: $[H^+] = 10^{-7}$ mol/L, neutral. Acidic: $[H^+] > 10^{-7}$ M.
  Basic: $[H^+] < 10^{-7}$ M.
- $pH = -\log[H^+]$.
