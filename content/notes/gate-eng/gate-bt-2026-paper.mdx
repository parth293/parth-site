## General Aptitude (Q1–10)

#### Q1 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** "He often \_\_\_\_\_ the numbers. False claims are not going to
help. Honesty \_\_\_\_\_ trust," said the manager. Choose the option with the
correct order of words to fill the blanks.

- (A) exaggerates; engenders
- (B) excels; encourages
- (C) aggravates; alleviates
- (D) diminishes; eliminates

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The second sentence ("False claims are not going to help")
tells us the first blank describes a dishonest habit with numbers — so the
first blank must mean overstates, not understates: rules out (C) and (D).
Between (A) and (B), "excels the numbers" isn't idiomatic (excel is
intransitive here), while "exaggerates the numbers" is exactly the dishonest
habit the sentence is warning about. For the second blank, "honesty
engenders trust" (engenders = brings about) is the standard sense — "honesty
encourages trust" is grammatically fine but weaker/less precise than the
intended pairing, and (A) reads cleanly as a matched pair.

**Answer: (A) exaggerates; engenders**

</details>

#### Q3 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** A school has 100 students distributed among 1st to 10th
standards. Which one of the following statements is always correct?

- (A) There are at least 10 students who belong to the same standard.
- (B) There is at least one student in each standard.
- (C) There are at most 10 students in 10th standard.
- (D) The total number of students from 1st to 5th standards is at least 50.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: Pigeonhole principle */}

$$
\text{at least one group has} \ge \left\lceil \frac{n}{k} \right\rceil \text{items}
$$

- $n$ — total number of items
- $k$ — number of groups the items are split into

This is a pigeonhole argument: 100 students spread over 10
standards average exactly 10 per standard, so at least one standard must
have 10 or more — if every standard had 9 or fewer, the total would be at
most 90 < 100, a contradiction. (B), (C), and (D) each describe one possible
distribution, not one forced by the numbers (e.g. all 100 students could be
in a single standard, satisfying (A) but violating (B), (C), and (D)).

**Answer: (A)**

</details>

#### Q4 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using three distinct
single-digit prime numbers?

- (A) 64
- (B) 24
- (C) 12
- (D) 4

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: Permutations (ordered selection, no repetition) */}

$$
P(n,r) = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}
$$

- $n$ — total number of items available
- $r$ — number chosen and arranged (order matters)

The single-digit primes are 2, 3, 5, 7 — four digits. A
3-digit number using three *distinct* ones is an ordered selection of 3 from
4 (order matters, since 235 ≠ 253), and none of these digits is 0 so every
arrangement is a valid 3-digit number. Count = $P(4,3) = 4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$.

**Answer: (B) 24**

</details>

#### Q5 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** In a group of students, 10 students like Mathematics, 12
students like English, 4 students like both Mathematics and English, and 6
students like neither. The number of students in the group is ____.

- (A) 18
- (B) 20
- (C) 24
- (D) 32

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: Inclusion–exclusion (two sets) */}

$$
|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|
$$

- $|A|$, $|B|$ — count of items with property $A$, with property $B$
- $|A \cap B|$ — count of items with both properties

By inclusion-exclusion, students who like at least one subject
= $10 + 12 - 4 = 18$. Adding the 6 who like neither: total $= 18 + 6 = 24$.

**Answer: (C) 24**

</details>

#### Q6 — 2 marks, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** Charity : P :: Retaliation : Q. Choose the appropriate pair of
words P and Q that fit the analogy.

- (A) P = Parsimonious; Q = Vengeful
- (B) P = Altruistic; Q = Amicable
- (C) P = Resentful; Q = Spiteful
- (D) P = Magnanimous; Q = Vindictive

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The analogy pairs an act with the trait of a person who
performs it: charity is done by someone who is magnanimous (generous), and
retaliation is done by someone who is vindictive (bent on revenge). (A)
pairs charity with its *opposite* trait (parsimonious = stingy), breaking
the pattern; (B) and (C) don't consistently match either act to its
performer's trait.

**Answer: (D) P = Magnanimous; Q = Vindictive**

</details>

#### Q7 — 2 marks, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** A paper shown in Panel I is folded along the dashed lines to
construct a cube; the shaded regions in Panel I appear on the outer surface
of the cube. Referring to the cubes in Panel II, which one of the options is
correct — (i) only, (ii) only, both, or neither?

![Panel I: a six-square net (a column of four squares, with one extra square attached to the right of the top square and one to the right of the bottom square), marked with a triangle, a solid square, a small square, a diamond, and two more triangles. Panel II shows two candidate folded cubes, (i) and (ii), each with a diamond on the front face and shading on the top and right faces.](/notes/gate-eng/gate-bt-2026-q7-cube-net.png)

- (A) Only (i) can correspond to the unfolded cube in Panel I.
- (B) Only (ii) can correspond to the unfolded cube in Panel I.
- (C) Both (i) and (ii) can correspond to the unfolded cube in Panel I.
- (D) Neither (i) nor (ii) can correspond to the unfolded cube in Panel I.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The diamond-square sits two folds away from the top-left square in both
directions, so it lands on the same face regardless of fold order, and
both rendered options correctly put it on the front face — that part
doesn't discriminate between (i) and (ii). The solid-black square and the
two corner triangles are what differ between them, and tracing exactly
which cube edge each lands on (by physically folding the net, or walking
through it face by face) shows only option (i) reproduces the shading in
Panel I correctly — (ii) has one of the corner triangles on the wrong
face/orientation.

**Answer: (A) Only (i)**

</details>

#### Q8 — 2 marks, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** Consider a cube with its 8 corners labelled a–h. All corners
are colored such that any two corners connected by an edge must be different
colors. The minimum number of colors required is ____.

![A cube with corners labelled a, b, c, d on the top face and e, f, g, h on the bottom face, edges connecting each top corner to the one below it](/notes/gate-eng/gate-bt-2026-q8-cube-graph.png)

- (A) 8
- (B) 4
- (C) 3
- (D) 2

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The cube's vertex-edge graph is bipartite: split the 8
corners into two sets by parity — e.g. label each corner by how many of its
3 binary coordinates are 1; every edge of a cube connects a vertex of even
parity to one of odd parity, never same-to-same. Coloring one parity class
one color and the other a second color satisfies the constraint, and one
color alone can't work since adjacent corners would clash — so the minimum
is 2.

**Answer: (D) 2**

</details>

#### Q9 — 2 marks, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** Four hills H1–H4 satisfy: (i) neither H2 nor H3 is
easternmost; (ii) neither H2 nor H3 is westernmost; (iii) neither the
easternmost nor the westernmost hill is the southernmost; (iv) two hills
are located to the west of H2; (v) the southernmost hill has at least two
hills to its east. The southernmost hill is ____.

- (A) H1
- (B) H2
- (C) H3
- (D) H4

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

From (i) and (ii), H2 and H3 occupy the two *middle*
west–east positions, leaving H1 and H4 to occupy the westernmost and
easternmost ends (in some order). From (iv), exactly two hills lie west of
H2, which fixes H2 at the 3rd position from the west; H3 then takes the 2nd
position. From (v), the southernmost hill must have ≥2 hills to its east,
which is only possible from the 1st or 2nd position (west–east). From (iii),
the southernmost hill can't be at the westernmost (1st) or easternmost (4th)
position. The only position satisfying both is the 2nd — which is H3.

**Answer: (C) H3**

</details>

#### Q10 — 2 marks, MCQ

{/* syllabus: general-aptitude */}

**Question.** Circle $C_1$ (center $O_1$, radius $r_1$) touches square VWXY
at points P and Q; circle $C_2$ (center $O_2$, radius $r_2$) touches the
square at points R and S. The two circles touch each other at T. Given
$r_1 = 1\text{ cm}$ and $\overline{VY} = \overline{VW} = 4\text{ cm}$, find
$r_2$.

![Square VWXY with circle C1 (radius r1 = 1 cm, center O1) inscribed in the bottom-left corner touching sides at P and Q, and circle C2 (radius r2, center O2) inscribed in the top-right corner touching sides at R and S, the two circles tangent to each other at T](/notes/gate-eng/gate-bt-2026-q10-circles-in-square.png)

- (A) $4 - 3\sqrt{2}$
- (B) $1 + 2\sqrt{2}$
- (C) $7 - 4\sqrt{2}$
- (D) $5 + 3\sqrt{2}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: External tangency of two circles */}

$$
|O_1O_2| = r_1 + r_2
$$

- $O_1, O_2$ — centers of the two circles
- $r_1, r_2$ — their radii (consistent length units)

1. **Set up coordinates.** Place the square so $W$ is at the origin. Reading
   off the figure, going around the square the corners are
   $W=(0,0)$, $X=(4,0)$, $Y=(4,4)$, $V=(0,4)$ — a $4\times4$ square, since
   $\overline{VW}=\overline{VY}=4\text{ cm}$ are two of its sides.

2. **Place $O_1$.** $C_1$ sits in the $W$ corner, tangent to the two sides
   that meet there ($WV$, the left side $x=0$, and $WX$, the bottom side
   $y=0$). A circle tangent to a line is at a distance equal to its radius
   from that line — so being tangent to *both* $x=0$ and $y=0$ pins its
   center at $(r_1, r_1)$. With $r_1=1$: $O_1=(1,1)$.

3. **Place $O_2$.** Same logic, opposite corner: $C_2$ sits in the $Y$
   corner, tangent to the top side ($y=4$) and the right side ($x=4$). Being
   at distance $r_2$ from each pins its center at
   $(4-r_2,\ 4-r_2)$ — $r_2$ *in* from each of those two sides.

4. **Use the tangency condition between the circles.** The two circles
   touch each other externally at $T$ (they're outside one another, just
   grazing at one point) — for two externally tangent circles, the distance
   between centers equals the sum of the radii:
   $$
   |O_1O_2| = r_1+r_2
   $$
   This is the one geometric fact that turns the picture into an equation
   in $r_2$ (everything else is just substitution).

5. **Write out the distance.** Using the distance formula between
   $O_1=(1,1)$ and $O_2=(4-r_2,\ 4-r_2)$:
   $$
   |O_1O_2| = \sqrt{(4-r_2-1)^2 + (4-r_2-1)^2} = \sqrt{2(3-r_2)^2} = \sqrt{2}\,(3-r_2)
   $$
   (both coordinate differences are the same, $3-r_2$, because $O_1$ and
   $O_2$ sit symmetrically on the square's diagonal — so the two squared
   terms are identical, and $\sqrt{2\cdot(\cdot)^2}$ simplifies to
   $\sqrt2$ times that quantity.)

6. **Set the two expressions for $|O_1O_2|$ equal** (step 4's requirement,
   using step 5's formula, with $r_1=1$):
   $$
   \sqrt{2}\,(3 - r_2) = 1 + r_2
   $$

7. **Solve for $r_2$.** Expand the left side, then collect every $r_2$ term
   on one side:
   $$
   3\sqrt{2} - \sqrt{2}\,r_2 = 1 + r_2
   $$
   $$
   3\sqrt{2} - 1 = r_2 + \sqrt{2}\,r_2 = r_2\left(1+\sqrt{2}\right)
   $$
   $$
   r_2 = \frac{3\sqrt{2}-1}{1+\sqrt{2}}
   $$

8. **Rationalize the denominator**, so the answer matches the form of the
   options (no surd on the bottom). Multiply top and bottom by
   $(\sqrt2-1)$ — chosen because $(1+\sqrt2)(\sqrt2-1)$ collapses to a plain
   number, clearing the denominator:
   $$
   (1+\sqrt{2})(\sqrt{2}-1) = \sqrt{2}-1+2-\sqrt{2} = 1
   $$
   $$
   (3\sqrt{2}-1)(\sqrt{2}-1) = 3\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2 - 3\sqrt2 - \sqrt2 + 1 = 6 - 4\sqrt{2} + 1 = 7-4\sqrt{2}
   $$
   So $r_2 = \dfrac{7-4\sqrt2}{1} = 7-4\sqrt{2}$.

9. **Sanity-check the number**, not just the algebra: $7-4\sqrt2 \approx
   7 - 5.657 = 1.34\text{ cm}$. That's positive (a radius has to be), and
   less than 3 cm (needed for step 5's $3-r_2$ to be positive, i.e. for
   $O_2$ to still be past $O_1$ along the diagonal) — both checks pass, so
   the answer is geometrically sensible, not just an artifact of the
   algebra.

**Answer: (C) $7 - 4\sqrt{2}$**

</details>

## BT Subject — one-mark MCQ (Q11–35)

#### Q11 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 1.2.1 */}

**Question.** Consider the two functions $f_1(x) = \dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ and
$f_2(x) = x^2 - 2x + 2$. Which of the following is the value of
$(f_1(x) + f_2(x))$ as $x \to 2$?

- (A) 0
- (B) 6
- (C) 8
- (D) $\infty$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$f_1$ has a removable discontinuity at $x=2$: factoring,
$f_1(x) = \dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = x+2$ for $x \neq 2$, so
$\lim_{x\to 2} f_1(x) = 4$. $f_2$ is a polynomial (continuous everywhere), so
$\lim_{x\to 2} f_2(x) = f_2(2) = 4-4+2 = 2$. Sum of the limits: $4+2=6$.

**Answer: (B) 6**

</details>

#### Q12 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 1.1.3 */}

**Question.** Which of the following is one of the eigenvalues for the
matrix given below?

$$
\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 4 \\ 4 & -3 \end{bmatrix}
$$

- (A) 1
- (B) 3
- (C) 5
- (D) 7

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: Eigenvalues of a matrix (characteristic equation) */}

$$
\det(A - \lambda I) = 0
$$

- $A$ — the square matrix
- $\lambda$ — an eigenvalue
- $I$ — the identity matrix of the same size as $A$

Solve $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$:
$(3-\lambda)(-3-\lambda) - 16 = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda^2 - 9 - 16 = 0
\Rightarrow \lambda^2 = 25 \Rightarrow \lambda = \pm 5$.

**Answer: (C) 5**

</details>

#### Q14 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 6.3.8 */}

**Question.** Cancer cells display a high level of heterogeneity in the
expression of TP53 protein. Which of the following techniques can be
directly used to determine the intra-tumoral heterogeneity of TP53
expression in a given tumor sample, without resorting to additional cell
separation techniques?

- (A) Immunoblotting
- (B) Flow cytometry
- (C) ELISA
- (D) Quantitative RT-PCR

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Immunoblotting, ELISA, and qRT-PCR all report a single bulk
average across the whole sample, which by construction cannot reveal
cell-to-cell heterogeneity. Flow cytometry measures TP53 signal in each
dissociated single cell and reports the full distribution across the
population — no upfront sorting/separation into subpopulations is needed to
see that heterogeneity, since it shows up directly as a spread in the
per-cell histogram.

**Answer: (B) Flow cytometry**

</details>

#### Q15 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 4.2.11 */}

**Question.** The transfer function of a Proportional-Integral (PI)
controller $G_C(s)$ is given by
$G_C(s) = K_C\left(1 + \dfrac{1}{\tau_I s}\right)$, where $K_C$ is the
controller gain, $\tau_I$ is the controller integral time constant, and $s$
is the Laplace variable. The role of the integral component of the
controller is to ____.

- (A) integrate the difference between the process and manipulated variable
- (B) integrate the difference between the set point and disturbance variable
- (C) integrate the difference between the set point and the measured variable
- (D) integrate the difference between the input and output variable

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: PI controller transfer function */}

$$
G_C(s) = K_C\left(1 + \frac{1}{\tau_I s}\right)
$$

- $K_C$ — controller gain
- $\tau_I$ — integral time constant ($\text{s}$ or $\text{min}$)
- $s$ — Laplace variable

A controller acts on the error signal, $e(t) = $ set point −
measured (process) variable. The integral term in a PI controller
accumulates (integrates) this error over time to drive out steady-state
offset — that's specifically the set point vs. measured-variable
difference, not the manipulated variable, disturbance, or a generic
input/output pair.

**Answer: (C)**

</details>

#### Q16 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 2.1.3 */}

**Question.** Which of the following enzymes is involved in the protection
of cells from reactive oxygen species?

- (A) Hexokinase
- (B) Glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase
- (C) Enolase
- (D) Pyruvate kinase

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) is the committed,
rate-limiting step of the pentose phosphate pathway, and its product is the
NADPH used to regenerate reduced glutathione (GSH) — the cell's main
buffer against reactive oxygen species. Hexokinase, enolase, and pyruvate
kinase are glycolytic enzymes with no direct role in redox defense.

**Answer: (B) Glucose 6-phosphate dehydrogenase**

</details>

#### Q17 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 2.2.6 */}

**Question.** In nitrogen fixation, to reduce nitrogen to ammonia, which one
of the following shows the correct order of electron flow?

- (A) Ferredoxin → reductase → nitrogenase
- (B) NADH → reductase → nitrogenase
- (C) Ferredoxin → nitrogenase → reductase
- (D) Reductase → NADH → nitrogenase

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

In the nitrogenase complex, ferredoxin donates electrons to
the Fe-protein (dinitrogenase reductase), which uses ATP hydrolysis to pass
those electrons one at a time to the MoFe-protein (dinitrogenase, i.e.
"nitrogenase" proper), which is where N₂ is actually reduced to ammonia.
So the order is ferredoxin → reductase → nitrogenase.

**Answer: (A)**

</details>

#### Q18 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 2.3.7 */}

**Question.** For monoclonal antibody production, hypoxanthine ____.

- (A) allows DNA synthesis in the absence of de novo dTMP synthesis
- (B) allows purine synthesis in cells containing HGPRT
- (C) induces cell fusion
- (D) prevents de novo nucleotide synthesis

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Hybridoma selection uses HAT medium (hypoxanthine,
aminopterin, thymidine). Aminopterin blocks de novo purine and thymidylate
synthesis, so surviving cells must run the salvage pathway instead.
Hypoxanthine feeds that salvage pathway specifically for purines, via
HGPRT (hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase) — myeloma fusion
partners are chosen to be HGPRT-deficient, so only fused hybridomas (which
inherit HGPRT from the B cell) can use hypoxanthine to survive. (A)
describes thymidine's role in the same medium, not hypoxanthine's.

**Answer: (B)**

</details>

#### Q19 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 4.2.6 */}

**Question.** Which one of the following proteins would elute third in gel
filtration chromatography from a mixture of four proteins given below?

- (A) Insulin (6 kDa)
- (B) Myoglobin (17 kDa)
- (C) Thrombin (37 kDa)
- (D) Albumin (66 kDa)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

In gel filtration (size-exclusion) chromatography, larger
proteins are excluded from the pores of the resin and elute first, while
smaller proteins get retained longer and elute last — the opposite order to
molecular weight. Elution order here is Albumin (66 kDa, 1st) → Thrombin
(37 kDa, 2nd) → Myoglobin (17 kDa, 3rd) → Insulin (6 kDa, 4th/last).

**Answer: (B) Myoglobin (17 kDa)**

</details>

#### Q20 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 2.2.8 */}

**Question.** Which one of the following antibiotics blocks protein chain
elongation by preventing the action of peptidyl transferase?

- (A) Bleomycin
- (B) Rifampicin
- (C) Chloramphenicol
- (D) Tetracycline

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Chloramphenicol binds the 50S ribosomal subunit and directly
inhibits peptidyl transferase, blocking peptide bond formation during
elongation. Bleomycin damages DNA (not a translation inhibitor); rifampicin
inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase (transcription, not translation); and
tetracycline blocks aminoacyl-tRNA from binding the ribosomal A site — a
different elongation-blocking mechanism, not peptidyl transferase
inhibition.

**Answer: (C) Chloramphenicol**

</details>

#### Q21 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 2.1.7 */}

**Question.** The catalytic efficiency of an enzyme following
Michaelis-Menten kinetics is defined by ____.

- (A) $k_{Cat}$
- (B) $V_{max} / k_{Cat}$
- (C) $k_{Cat} / K_M$
- (D) $k_{Cat} / V_{max}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Catalytic efficiency */}

$$
\text{Catalytic efficiency} = \frac{k_{cat}}{K_M}
$$

- $k_{cat}$ — turnover number ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $K_M$ — Michaelis constant ($\text{mol L}^{-1}$)

Catalytic efficiency is the standard Michaelis-Menten figure
of merit for how effectively an enzyme converts substrate at low
(non-saturating) concentration, defined as $k_{cat}/K_M$ — $k_{cat}$ alone
just measures turnover rate at saturation, without accounting for how
tightly the enzyme binds substrate ($K_M$).

**Answer: (C) $k_{Cat}/K_M$**

</details>

#### Q22 — 1 mark, MCQ

{/* syllabus: 3.3.4 */}

**Question.** The RNA primer synthesized during bacterial DNA replication
is removed by ____.

- (A) DNA gyrase
- (B) DNA Polymerase I
- (C) Primase
- (D) DNA Polymerase III

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Primase (C) *lays down* the RNA primer, and DNA Polymerase
III (D) extends it into new DNA — neither removes it. DNA gyrase (A)
relieves supercoiling ahead of the replication fork. DNA Polymerase I
carries a 5'→3' exonuclease activity that excises the RNA primer and then
fills the resulting gap with DNA, before DNA ligase seals the nick.

#### Q34 — 1 mark, NAT

{/* syllabus: 2.1.5 */}

**Question.** Given below is the Gibbs free energy change (ΔG) in kilo
Joules per electron equivalent (kJ e⁻eq⁻¹) at pH 7.0, of organic and
inorganic half reactions.

Acetate synthesis:
$$
\tfrac{1}{8}\text{CO}_2 + \tfrac{1}{8}\text{HCO}_3^- + \text{H}^+ + e^-
\rightarrow \tfrac{1}{8}\text{CH}_3\text{COO}^- + \tfrac{3}{8}\text{H}_2\text{O}
\quad \Delta G = 27.4\ \text{kJ e}^-\text{eq}^{-1}
$$

Reduction reaction:
$$
\tfrac{1}{4}\text{O}_2 + \text{H}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}
\quad \Delta G = -78.72\ \text{kJ e}^-\text{eq}^{-1}
$$

The free energy change of acetate oxidation to CO₂, H₂O and HCO₃⁻ is
____ kJ e⁻eq⁻¹. (rounded off to two decimal places)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Overall reaction free energy from half-reactions */}

$$
\Delta G_{rxn} = \Delta G_{donor\,(oxidation)} + \Delta G_{acceptor\,(reduction)}
$$

- $\Delta G_{donor}$ — the electron-donor half-reaction, written as an oxidation
- $\Delta G_{acceptor}$ — the electron-acceptor half-reaction, written as a reduction

This is asking for a full aerobic oxidation reaction, so it needs *both*
half-reactions combined, not just the acetate one on its own — that's why
the O₂ half-reaction is given at all.

1. **Acetate is the electron donor, and it's being oxidized** — the reverse
   of the given "acetate synthesis" (reduction) half-reaction. Reversing a
   half-reaction flips the sign of its ΔG:
   $$
   \Delta G_{\text{acetate oxidation}} = -27.4\ \text{kJ e}^-\text{eq}^{-1}
   $$

2. **O₂ is the electron acceptor, and it's being reduced** — exactly as
   given, no sign flip needed:
   $$
   \Delta G_{\text{O}_2\text{ reduction}} = -78.72\ \text{kJ e}^-\text{eq}^{-1}
   $$

3. **Combine the two per-electron-equivalent half-reactions into the
   overall reaction.** Because both are already normalized to one electron
   equivalent, the overall reaction's free energy is simply their sum (the
   electron donor supplies the electron, the acceptor consumes it — add the
   two ΔG's to get the net driving force of the coupled redox reaction):
   $$
   \Delta G_{\text{overall}} = \Delta G_{\text{donor (oxidation)}} + \Delta G_{\text{acceptor (reduction)}}
   = -27.4 + (-78.72) = -106.12\ \text{kJ e}^-\text{eq}^{-1}
   $$

**Answer: -106.12**

</details>

possible polypeptide(s) that can be hypothetically translated in the
cytosol and/or mitochondria, from the given full length mRNA sequence (36
nucleotides) is/are:

$$
\text{5'-AACACCAUGACCCAUGUGGCGAGACGGUAGUUAAAA-3'}

- (A) 7 amino acids long in the cytosol
- (B) 5 amino acids long in the mitochondria
- (C) 6 amino acids long in the mitochondria
- (D) 8 amino acids long in the cytosol

<summary>Solution</summary>

each needs to be checked separately as a possible start:

longer than the 6-aa alternative from start 2. In the mitochondria, the
AGA-truncated 5-aa product from start 1 is *not* the longest available,
because start 2 avoids the AGA stop entirely and reaches 6 amino acids
instead (C) — so (B) describes a real but non-maximal product, and isn't
selected.

- (A) The culture has died due to absence of oxygen
- (B) The culture has run out of carbon source
- (C) The culture growth becomes oxygen limited
- (D) The culture growth is independent of dissolved oxygen

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

A DO reading of zero *under continuous aeration* means oxygen is being
consumed as fast as (or faster than) it's being supplied — the oxygen
transfer rate can't keep up with the culture's oxygen uptake rate, so

culture being dead (A — cells can grow perfectly well right up to the

nothing about carbon source (B — an unrelated, unsupported inference), and
directly contradicts (D).

**Answer: (C)**

#### Q46 — 2 marks, MSQ

{/* syllabus: 4.1.10 */}

**Question.** In an aerobic fermentation with air sparging, which of the
following options can be used to increase the volumetric mass transfer
coefficient for oxygen transfer?

- (A) Using pure oxygen in place of air
- (B) Increasing the volumetric flow rate of air
- (C) Reducing the bubble diameter
- (D) Decreasing the agitation rate

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$k_La$ is a physical/hydrodynamic property of the gas-liquid system — how
much interfacial area is available and how fast oxygen crosses it — not a
statement about the driving force. Switching to pure oxygen (A) increases

#### Q47 — 2 marks, MSQ

{/* syllabus: 4.1.10 */}

**Question.** For a liquid flowing in a circular cross section pipe, the
transition from laminar to turbulent flow will NOT depend on which of the
following factor(s)?

- (A) The diameter of the pipe
- (B) The length of the pipe
- (C) The linear velocity of the liquid
- (D) The viscosity of the liquid

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Reynolds number */}

$$
Re = \frac{\rho v D}{\mu}
$$

- $\rho$ — fluid density
- $v$ — velocity
- $D$ — pipe diameter
- $\mu$ — viscosity

transitions at the same $Re$, so length is the one factor that doesn't
matter.

#### Q50 — 2 marks, MSQ

{/* syllabus: 2.1.7 */}

**Question.** An enzyme-catalyzed reaction is found to have
$\Delta G = -100\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$. Which of the following statements
about this reaction is/are true?

- (A) The rate of the reaction cannot be predicted
- (B) The rate of the reaction is high
- (C) The rate of the reaction is low
- (D) The reaction is irreversible

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Equilibrium constant from Gibbs free energy */}

$$
K_{eq} = e^{-\Delta G/RT}
$$

- $\Delta G$ — standard Gibbs free energy change
- $R$ — gas constant
- $T$ — absolute temperature

$\Delta G$ is a *thermodynamic* quantity — it tells you the direction and
extent of a reaction at equilibrium, not how fast it gets there. Rate is
governed by kinetics (activation energy, $k_{cat}$), which $\Delta G$ says

kinetically slow (that's exactly why cells need enzymes at all). So (A) is
correct, and (B)/(C) are both unjustified guesses in either direction.
Separately, $K_{eq} = e^{-\Delta G/RT}$: for $\Delta G=-100\text{ kJ/mol}$
at room temperature, $K_{eq}$ is astronomically large, meaning the reverse
reaction is negligible — thermodynamically, that's effectively
irreversible (D), even though nothing here says anything about its rate.

**Answer: (A) and (D)**

</details>

#### Q51 — 2 marks, NAT

{/* syllabus: 1.5.2 */}

**Question.** Consider the equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{1}{x}$. The
value of the integral $\displaystyle\int_1^2 dy$ using the trapezoidal
method and interval $h=0.25$ is ________. (rounded off to two decimal
places)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* formula-name: Trapezoidal rule (numerical integration) */}

$$
\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{h}{2}\Big[f(x_0)+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_i)+f(x_n)\Big]
$$

- $h$ — step size between points
- $x_0,\dots,x_n$ — equally spaced points from $a$ to $b$

Since $dy/dx = 1/x$, the integral $\int_1^2 dy$ is really
$\int_1^2 \frac{1}{x}\,dx$, approximated numerically with 4 trapezoidal
strips ($h=0.25$, points $x=1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2$):

| $x$ | 1 | 1.25 | 1.5 | 1.75 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $f(x)=1/x$ | 1 | 0.8 | 0.6667 | 0.5714 | 0.5 |

$$
\int_1^2 \frac{dx}{x} \approx \frac{h}{2}\Big[f(x_0) + 2\big(f(x_1)+f(x_2)+f(x_3)\big) + f(x_4)\Big]
= \frac{0.25}{2}\big[1 + 2(2.0381) + 0.5\big] = 0.697
$$

(Sanity check: the exact value is $\ln 2 = 0.6931$ — trapezoidal
over-estimates slightly here because $1/x$ is convex, which matches.)

**Answer: 0.70**

</details>

#### Q54 — 2 marks, NAT

{/* syllabus: 1.3.1 */}

**Question.** A heat exchanger during operation in a bioprocess has a
steady temperature of 90°C. After completion of its operation, it was
shut down and it was observed that the rate of decrease of temperature at
any time was directly proportional to the difference $T(t)-30\text{°C}$,
where $T(t)$ denotes temperature at time $t$. It was observed that it
took 30 min for the temperature to drop to 70°C. The temperature after
51.5 min will be ________ °C. (rounded off to the nearest integer)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Newton's law of cooling */}

$$
T(t) = T_{env} + (T_0-T_{env})e^{-kt}
$$

- $T_{env}$ — ambient/final temperature
- $T_0$ — initial temperature
- $k$ — cooling rate constant

This is Newton's law of cooling: $\frac{dT}{dt}=-k(T-30)$, giving
$T(t)-30 = (T_0-30)e^{-kt} = 60\,e^{-kt}$ with $T_0=90$.

1. **Find $k$ from the given data point** ($T=70$ at $t=30$):
   $$

   $$

2. **Evaluate at $t=51.5$ min:**
   $$
   T-30 = 60\,e^{-0.01352 \times 51.5} = 60\,e^{-0.6960} \approx 60(0.4985) \approx 29.9
   $$
   $$

   $$

#### Q55 — 2 marks, NAT

{/* syllabus: 4.1.6 */}

**Question.** A microbial culture (following Monod model for growth) has
a maximum specific growth rate of $0.1\text{ h}^{-1}$, Monod constant of
$1\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ and endogenous decay rate of $0.1\text{ day}^{-1}$.
At limiting substrate concentration of $23\text{ g L}^{-1}$, the net
specific growth rate will be ________ day⁻¹. (rounded off to one decimal
place)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Monod kinetics with endogenous decay */}

$$
\mu_{net} = \mu_{max}\frac{S}{K_S+S} - k_d
$$

- $\mu_{max}$ — maximum specific growth rate
- $K_S$ — Monod (half-saturation) constant
- $S$ — limiting substrate concentration
- $k_d$ — endogenous decay rate

Net specific growth rate accounts for endogenous decay:
$\mu_{net} = \mu_{max}\dfrac{S}{K_S+S} - k_d$. Everything needs to be in
the same time base (days) before combining terms:

1. **Convert $\mu_{max}$ to day⁻¹:** $0.1\text{ h}^{-1} \times 24 = 2.4\text{ day}^{-1}$.
2. **Evaluate the Monod term**, with $S=23{,}000\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ and
   $K_S=1\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (converting $S$ to the same units as $K_S$):
   $$
   \mu = 2.4 \times \frac{23{,}000}{1+23{,}000} \approx 2.4 \times 0.99996 \approx 2.4\text{ day}^{-1}
   $$
   ($S \gg K_S$ here, so the culture is essentially at $\mu_{max}$.)
3. **Subtract decay:** $\mu_{net} = 2.4 - 0.1 = 2.3\text{ day}^{-1}$.

**Answer: 2.3**

</details>

#### Q56 — 2 marks, NAT

{/* syllabus: 4.1.9 */}

**Question.** A CSTR with a volume of $100\text{ m}^3$ is operated in

$$

$$

- $V$ — reactor volume
- $X$ — in-reactor biomass concentration
- $Q$ — flow rate of the stream leaving the system
- $X_e$ — biomass concentration in that leaving stream

**Question.** A packed bed bioreactor with length 1 m and inside diameter

fraction of the packed bed is ________. (rounded off to two decimal
places)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

{/* importance: very-important */}
{/* formula-name: Superficial vs. interstitial velocity (packed bed) */}

$$
v_{interstitial} = \frac{v_{superficial}}{\varepsilon}, \qquad v_{superficial}=\frac{Q}{A}
$$

- $\varepsilon$ — bed void fraction
- $Q$ — volumetric flow rate
- $A$ — empty-column cross-sectional area

The **superficial velocity** is the flow rate divided by the full
(empty-column) cross-sectional area — the void fraction $\varepsilon$ is
what relates it to the **interstitial velocity** (the actual, faster
velocity through the pores): $v_{interstitial} = v_{superficial}/\varepsilon$.

1. **Cross-sectional area** (diameter 10 cm, radius 5 cm):
   $A = \pi r^2 = \pi(5)^2 = 78.54\text{ cm}^2$.
2. **Superficial velocity:** $v_s = Q/A = 25\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1} / 78.54\text{ cm}^2 = 0.318\text{ cm s}^{-1}$
   (converting $Q=0.025\text{ L s}^{-1}=25\text{ cm}^3\text{s}^{-1}$).
3. **Solve for $\varepsilon$:**
   $$
   \varepsilon = \frac{v_s}{v_{interstitial}} = \frac{0.318}{1} \approx 0.32
   $$

**Answer: 0.32**
