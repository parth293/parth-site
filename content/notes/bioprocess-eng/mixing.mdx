## Why mixing matters in bioreactors

Mixing creates a homogeneous environment in the bioreactor, needed for:

- Dispersing gases.
- Promoting heat exchange.
- Suspension of solid particles.
- Dispersing immiscible liquids.

## Continuous stirred tank reactor (CSTR)

To achieve the goal of mixing, the design of a continuous stirred tank
reactor (CSTR) is studied, understanding its different components.

### General design ratios

| Component | Ratio | Typical range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impeller diameter | $D_i / D_t$ | $1/3$ – $1/2$ (range for good mixing) |
| Liquid height | $H_L / D_t$ | $1$ – $1.25$ |
| Baffle width | $W_b / D_t$ | $1/10$ – $1/12$ |

- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)
- $D_t$ — tank diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)
- $H_L$ — height of liquid in the tank, in metres ($\text{m}$)
- $W_b$ — baffle width, in metres ($\text{m}$)

### Headspace

The tank should never be filled completely with liquid, because gases are
being released by microbes. Without that headspace, the gas released can
cause pressure to rise and the tank to burst.

## Parts of a bioreactor

### Stirrer shaft

The rod on which the impeller blades are attached, and which is itself
attached to a motor. The motor powers and rotates the shaft, and the shaft
rotates the blades.

### Baffles

**Baffles** are vertical metal sheets or strips attached to the inside of the
tank, used to:

- Break circulatory flow of the fluid.
- Reduce gross vortex formation.
- Produce turbulence and eddies.
- Help exchange heat (coils for heat exchange are arranged around them).

![Cutaway of a cylindrical bioreactor tank showing vertical baffle plates mounted against the inside wall](/notes/bioprocess-eng/baffle-arrangement-cutaway.png)

A typical reactor uses 4 baffles.

### Sparger (aerator)

A **sparger** introduces gases into the liquid medium.

![Diagram of sparger designs and a schematic comparing three gas sparger types: perforated plate, ladder distributor, and tri-nozzle sparger](/notes/bioprocess-eng/gas-sparger-types.png)

| Sparger type | Used at | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perforated plate | Lab scale | Con: pores get blocked by fungal mycelia |
| Tri-nozzle sparger / ladder distributor (ladder sparger) | Industrial scale | Large pores — don't get blocked |

## Power consumption

While mixing is important, bioreactors cannot be mixed at full speed. As
power increases, shear stress on cells in the bulk fluid increases, and
beyond a certain point the cells get lysed or damaged. This risk is highest
for eukaryotes, which have no cell wall — prokaryotes and plants do have
cell walls.

### Units of power

One watt equals:

- $1\ \text{J/s}$ (one joule per second)
- $1\ \text{N·m/s}$ (one newton-metre per second)
- $1\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}^3$ (one kilogram metre-squared per second-cubed)

### Power number (Newton number, $N_p$)

**Power number** ($N_p$) indicates the power required for agitating
non-aerated fluids. In turbulent flow, $N_p$ is a constant depending on the
type of impeller used; in laminar flow, $N_p$ is not constant.

$$
N_p = \frac{P}{\rho N_i^3 D_i^5}
$$

- $N_p$ — power number (Newton number), dimensionless
- $P$ — power, in watts ($\text{W}$)
- $\rho$ — density of the fluid, in kilograms per cubic metre ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
- $N_i$ — impeller rotational speed, in reciprocal seconds ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

**Before solving any problem involving $N_p$, always assess whether the flow
is turbulent or laminar first** — $N_p$ is only constant in turbulent flow.

### Power in laminar flow

In laminar flow, power is given by:

$$
P = K_L \mu N_i^2 D_i^3
$$

- $P$ — power, in watts ($\text{W}$)
- $K_L$ — laminar power constant, a dimensionless constant depending on the
  geometry of the impeller and tank (blade pitch, blade shape, number of
  blades, vessel geometry)
- $\mu$ — dynamic viscosity of the fluid, in pascal-seconds ($\text{Pa} \cdot \text{s}$)
- $N_i$ — impeller rotational speed, in reciprocal seconds ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

## Impeller tip speed

![Top-down view of a three-blade impeller rotating, with tip speed vectors shown tangential to each blade tip](/notes/bioprocess-eng/impeller-tip-speed-diagram.png)

$$
v_{tip} = \pi N_i D_i
$$

- $v_{tip}$ — impeller tip speed, in metres per second ($\text{m/s}$)
- $N_i$ — impeller rotational speed, in reciprocal seconds ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

## Flow number ($F_l$ / $F_Q$)

The effectiveness of the impeller for pumping the fluid is represented by a
dimensionless number, the **flow number**.

$$
F_l = \frac{Q}{N_i D_i^3}
$$

- $F_l$ — flow number (also written $F_Q$), dimensionless
- $Q$ — volumetric flow rate of fluid leaving the impeller blade, in cubic metres per second ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$)
- $N_i$ — impeller rotational speed, in reciprocal seconds ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

### Common confusion: power number vs. flow number

Both are built from the same $\rho$, $N_i$, $D_i$ terms and are easy to swap
under exam pressure — the difference is what they measure and the power on
$D_i$.

| | Power number ($N_p$) | Flow number ($F_l$) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formula | $N_p = P/(\rho N_i^3 D_i^5)$ | $F_l = Q/(N_i D_i^3)$ |
| Represents | Power drawn to agitate the fluid | Pumping capacity (volumetric throughput) |
| Power on $D_i$ | $5$ | $3$ |
| High value means | High shear / power draw (e.g. Rushton) | High bulk turnover (e.g. axial impellers) |

## Mixing time

Mixing time should be as low as possible. If mixing time is high, when
chemicals are added, the local region they are added to can shift in pH or
other local conditions enough that the cells there die.

### Calculating mixing time ($t_m$ / $T_m$)

#### If power is given

$$
t_m = 5.9\, D_T^{2/3} \left(\frac{\rho V_L}{P}\right)^{1/3} \left(\frac{D_T}{D_i}\right)^{1/3}
$$

- $t_m$ — mixing time, in seconds ($\text{s}$)
- $D_T$ — tank diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)
- $\rho$ — density of the fluid, in kilograms per cubic metre ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
- $V_L$ — volume of liquid, in cubic metres ($\text{m}^3$)
- $P$ — power, in watts ($\text{W}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

#### If power number is given

$$
t_m = \frac{5.4}{N_i} \left(\frac{1}{N_p}\right)^{1/3} \left(\frac{D_T}{D_i}\right)^2
$$

- $t_m$ — mixing time, in seconds ($\text{s}$)
- $N_i$ — impeller rotational speed, in reciprocal seconds ($\text{s}^{-1}$)
- $N_p$ — power number, dimensionless
- $D_T$ — tank diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)
- $D_i$ — impeller diameter, in metres ($\text{m}$)

## Impeller

### Axial flow vs. radial flow impellers

**Axial flow impellers** discharge fluid primarily parallel to the impeller
drive shaft, along the vertical axis, creating top-to-bottom bulk turnover.
**Radial flow impellers** discharge fluid perpendicular to the drive shaft,
radially outward to the vessel wall, where it splits into two circulation
loops — one moving upward, one moving downward.

![Axial flow impeller pushing fluid along the vertical shaft axis for top-to-bottom turnover, versus radial flow impeller pushing fluid outward toward the vessel wall in two circulation loops](/notes/bioprocess-eng/axial-vs-radial-flow-impellers.png)

| Feature | Axial flow impeller | Radial flow impeller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discharge direction | Parallel to drive shaft (downward/axial) | Perpendicular to shaft (outward to walls) |
| Primary mechanism | High volumetric pumping rate ($Q$) | High localized shear stress ($\tau$) |
| Turbulent power number ($N_p$) | Low ($N_p \approx 0.3$–$1.5$) | High ($N_p \approx 4.5$–$6.0$) |
| Shear sensitivity | Low shear (gentle mixing) | High shear (aggressive breakup) |
| Gas dispersion capability | Poor (prone to gas cavity flooding) | Excellent (effective bubble breakup, high $k_L a$) |
| Primary process use | Cell suspension, animal/plant cell culture, blending | Aerobic bacterial fermentation, gas-liquid mass transfer |
| Standard examples | Marine propeller, pitched-blade turbine (PBT), hydrofoil (Lightnin A310) | Rushton 6-flat-blade turbine, paddle agitator |

### Types of impellers

| Impeller | Flow | Shear | $N_p$ | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rushton turbine | Radial | High | $\approx 5.0$ | Aerobic microbial fermentation — top $O_2$ transfer |
| Marine propeller | Axial | Low | $0.3$–$0.5$ | Mammalian / plant cell culture |
| Hydrofoil (A310) | Axial | Low–moderate | $0.25$–$0.3$ | Energy-efficient mixing, microbial + cell culture |
| Pitched blade turbine | Axial | Moderate | — | Viscous fluids, suspensions, enzymatic reactions |
| Anchor | Tangential | Low | — | High-viscosity, wall-scraping (gels, creams) |
| Helical ribbon | Axial / helical | Low | — | Very high-viscosity, non-Newtonian fluids |

#### Rushton turbine

![Rushton turbine impeller, a radial-flow impeller with flat blades mounted on a central disc](/notes/bioprocess-eng/rushton-turbine.png)

Highest shear of the six — not recommended for animal cells (shear-sensitive,
no cell wall).

#### Marine propeller

![Large marine propeller mounted on a ship hull, an axial-flow impeller design](/notes/bioprocess-eng/marine-propeller.png)

Gentle axial flow — default choice for delicate (mammalian/plant) cultures.

#### Hydrofoil impeller (e.g., A310)

![Hydrofoil impeller with three curved, low-pitch blades for energy-efficient axial flow](/notes/bioprocess-eng/hydrofoil-impeller-a310.png)

Lowest power draw of the axial impellers — efficient mixing at moderate
shear.

#### Pitched blade turbine

![Pitched blade turbine impeller with four flat blades angled off the horizontal for up- or down-pumping flow](/notes/bioprocess-eng/pitched-blade-turbine.png)

Only impeller here that runs in both up-pumping and down-pumping
configurations.

#### Anchor impeller

![Anchor impeller shaped to match the curvature of the tank wall, used to scrape the vessel wall](/notes/bioprocess-eng/anchor-impeller.png)

Blades match the tank wall's curvature and scrape it directly — the only one
here built to prevent wall buildup.

#### Helical ribbon impeller

![Helical ribbon impeller with a ribbon blade wound in a spiral around the central shaft](/notes/bioprocess-eng/helical-ribbon-impeller.png)

Ribbon blade spirals around the shaft — for the thickest, most
viscosity-challenged fluids of the six (polymerization mixtures, biomass
slurries).

## Key points

- **Mixing time** should be minimized — a slow mix lets local pH or
  concentration spikes near the addition point kill nearby cells.
- **Baffles** break circulatory flow and vortex formation, converting it
  into turbulence and eddies; a standard reactor uses 4.
- **Power number** ($N_p$) is constant only in turbulent flow — in laminar
  flow it depends on Reynolds number, so always check the flow regime first.
- **Flow number** ($F_l$) scales with $D_i^3$ while **power number** scales
  with $D_i^5$ — the two formulas look alike but respond very differently to
  impeller size.
- Radial (Rushton) impellers give high shear and high $O_2$ transfer; axial
  impellers (propeller, hydrofoil, pitched blade) give low-to-moderate shear
  and high pumping — never use Rushton for shear-sensitive cultures.
- Viscosity ladder for impeller choice: pitched blade → anchor → helical
  ribbon, as fluids get thicker and more non-Newtonian; anchor is the only
  one built to scrape the tank wall.
