## Chapter 1: The New Rules

### Steps to follow

1. Pause
2. Blink
3. Mindfully urge heart rate back to normal
4. Long, slow stare
5. "How am I supposed to do that?"
6. "I'm sorry Robert, how do I even know he's alive?"
7. "I really am sorry, but how can I get you any money, let alone a million
   dollars, if I don't even know he's alive?"

### Personal identity

- "I've always thought of myself as just a regular guy. Hardworking and
  willing to learn, yes, but not particularly talented."

### Voice

- The best way to describe the late-night FM DJ's voice is as the voice of
  calm and reason.

### The smartest dumb guy in the room

- "We've got your son, Voss. Give us one million dollars or he dies."
  - "We've got your son, Voss. Give us one million dollars or he dies,"
    Mnookin said, smiling. "I'm the kidnapper. What are you going to do?"
  - Pause. Blink. Mindfully urge the heart rate back to normal.
  - "C'mon. Get me the money or I cut your son's throat right now," Mnookin
    said.
  - I gave him a long, slow stare. Then I smiled. "How am I supposed to do
    that?"
  - "So you're okay with me killing your son, Mr. Voss?"
  - "I'm sorry, Robert, how do I know he's even alive?" — using an apology and
    his first name, seeding warmth into the interaction to complicate his
    gambit to bulldoze me.
  - "I really am sorry, but how can I get you any money right now, much less
    one million dollars, if I don't even know he's alive?"

### The FBI gets emotional

- Human beings are fundamentally irrational. That's why the techniques
  written in *Getting to Yes* don't typically work.
- People want to be understood and accepted.
- Listening is how we can get there.
- By listening intensely, a negotiator demonstrates a sincere desire to
  better understand what the other side is experiencing.
- When people feel listened to, they tend to listen to themselves more
  carefully, and to openly evaluate and clarify their own thoughts and
  feelings.
- They also tend to become less defensive and more willing to listen to
  other points of view, which gets them to the calm, logical place where
  they can be good *Getting to Yes* problem solvers.

### Life is negotiation

- The first step is to get over your aversion to negotiating.
- You don't need to like it — you just need to understand that's how the
  world works.
- Negotiating does not mean browbeating or grinding someone down. It simply
  means playing the emotional game that human society is set up for.
- In this world, you get what you ask for; you just have to ask correctly.
  So claim your right to ask for what you think is right.
- Negotiation is the heart of collaboration. It is what makes conflict
  potentially meaningful and productive for all parties.
- It can change your life, as it has changed mine.
